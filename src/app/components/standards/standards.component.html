Qualification Guidance based on the 12 Standards

Since the 12 standards are broad, inspiring, and intertwined, the qualification approach should emphasize the depth demonstrated in one and the alignment with the spirit of the others.

Minimum Qualification

An element qualifies if it clearly satisfies at least one of the 12 standards with strong and verifiable evidence (e.g., living traditions, cultural transmission, democratic practice, etc.), while also aligning, in principle, with the broader set of standards.

This ensures the inclusion of diverse elements, ranging from oral traditions to cooperative institutions.

Recognition of High Significance

Elements that substantially satisfy 5 or more standards (across at least 3 thematic groups: Origins and Continuity, Democracy and Belonging, Community and Transmission, Solidarity and Global Significance) may be recognized as having regional or international significance.

Alternatively

An element must demonstrate alignment with at least 4 standards, including:

  • • Origins or Traditions (e.g., foundations, early cooperatives, living practices)
  • • Democracy and Belonging (e.g., participation, identity, intergenerational solidarity)
  • • Transmission or Innovation (e.g., education, storytelling, renewal)
  • • Community or Global Significance (e.g., resilience, sustainability, solidarity beyond borders)

Exemplary Models

Initiatives that satisfy 8 or more standards and demonstrate continuity and innovation should be recognized as global examples of Cooperative Cultural Heritage, showcasing exceptional universal value.
